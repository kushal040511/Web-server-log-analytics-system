How it was validated

Features used

Why a Gaussian Mixture Model and not K-Means. Automated hosts are a fraction of a percent of all hosts. K-Means minimises total within-cluster variance and assigns hard labels, so a group that small either gets absorbed into a large cluster or shattered into fragments — at k=2 it performed worse than random. GMM fits a full covariance matrix per component, so it can model a small, elongated cluster, and it returns a membership probability per host rather than a yes-or-no label. Across a sweep of k from 2 to 8, GMM beat both K-Means and Bisecting K-Means at every recall level.
